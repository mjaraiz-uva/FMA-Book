Free-market agent-based modeling quantifies and forecasts selection and evolution

Martin Jaraiz

Department of Electronics, University of Valladolid, 47011 Valladolid, Spain

Abstract — A Free-Market Agent-Based Model (FM-ABM) implements Assembly Theory's forward dynamics through Darwinian economic optimization. Where Assembly Theory (AT) measures selection post-hoc through assembly index and copy number1, FM-ABM provides the explicit mechanism: supply, demand, and competition among economic agents enacting chemistry. Starting from bare C, H, O, N atoms with Bond Dissociation Energy (BDE) and Universal Force Field (UFF) physics, the model discovers 10 of 12 feasible amino acids in minutes on a laptop — the same “early 10” that Miller–Urey experiments2, meteorite analysis3, and phylogenetics4 independently identify. We demonstrate that AT's three τd/τp regimes (stagnation, selection, tar) emerge naturally from a single parameter (discovery budget), and that the non-monotonic copy-number signature AT predicts as evidence of selection appears spontaneously through market dynamics. FM-ABM provides what AT identifies as missing: a physics-grounded forward process that distinguishes “novel functional features from random fluctuations” through neighbor interaction (exploration) and consumption (selection). Where AT explains and quantifies, FM-ABM quantifies and forecasts.

1. Introduction

Assembly Theory (AT) offers a powerful measurement framework for detecting selection in molecular systems: objects with high assembly index found in high copy number cannot arise from undirected physics alone1. Yet AT's authors acknowledge a fundamental gap:

“Physics has no functional view of the Universe — it cannot distinguish novel functional features from random fluctuations.”1

We present a computational system that fills this gap. The Free-Market Agent-Based Model (FM-ABM) distinguishes novelty from noise through two concrete mechanisms:

  1. Natural interaction with neighbors = exploration. When a worker's molecule meets a neighbor's molecule and they combine exothermically, a genuinely new product is created. This is AT's discovery dynamics, grounded in real thermodynamics.
  2. Consumption of the new product = selection. If the new product satisfies market demand, it gets produced repeatedly. If not, it decays. Market demand is functional evaluation — AT's production dynamics made concrete.

This is not an abstract mathematical exercise. FM-ABM operates on real chemistry with real energies — Bond Dissociation Energies for thermodynamic feasibility, Universal Force Field for 3D geometry validation, and Evans–Polanyi kinetic barriers for reaction rates. Starting from bare atoms (C, H, O, N), the model spontaneously discovers amino acid synthesis pathways, reproducing the hierarchy observed in prebiotic experiments, meteorites, and phylogenetic reconstruction.

The FM-ABM framework was originally developed for economic simulation as the DEPLOYERS framework5,6, and has been shown to forecast macroeconomic variables such as GDP7. The application to chemistry exploits a deep isomorphism: atoms are goods, reactions are production functions, and thermodynamic selection is market selection. The universality of this mapping suggests that free-market dynamics may constitute a fundamental mechanism for selection across domains.

Central thesis: Assembly Theory provides the WHAT (measuring selection via assembly index + copy number). FM-ABM provides the HOW (generating selection through supply, demand, and competition). Where AT explains and quantifies, FM-ABM quantifies and forecasts: given initial conditions (atoms, temperature, discovery budget), it predicts what will be selected.

2. Assembly Theory: Key Concepts

We briefly summarize the AT constructs most relevant to our work, following Sharma et al.1.

2.1 Assembly Index and Copy Number

The assembly index ai of an object is the minimum number of recursive joining operations needed to construct it from a set of basic building blocks. For molecules, these blocks are atoms or simple fragments; the index counts the shortest path through “assembly space” from primitives to the target.

The copy number ni counts how many identical copies of an object exist in a sample. AT's key insight is that objects with both high ai and high ni require an explanation beyond undirected physics.

2.2 The Assembly Equation

A  =  Σ  eai × (ni − 1)  /  NT

The exponential weighting ensures that even modest copy numbers of high-index objects contribute enormously to the assembly A. This makes A a sensitive detector of selection.

2.3 The Selection Parameter α

AT parameterizes selection through α in the production probability:

Pa  ∝  (Na)α

where Na is the number of objects at assembly index a. When α = 1, production is undirected (proportional to abundance). When α < 1, production is directed — rarer, higher-index objects are preferentially produced. AT demonstrates that α < 1 generates non-monotonic copy-number distributions, the signature of selection.

2.4 Three Dynamical Regimes

AT identifies three regimes based on the ratio of production timescale τp to discovery timescale τd:

Stagnation

τp << τd

Production outpaces discovery. Few species, but those that exist are amplified. Selection signature visible.

Selection

τd ≈ τp

Balanced exploration and exploitation. Maximal diversity with sustained production. The “sweet spot.”

Tar

τp >> τd

Discovery outpaces production. Combinatorial explosion — everything is discovered, nothing persists.

2.5 AT's Acknowledged Limitation

“We are putting selection in by hand in our examples to demonstrate foundational principles.”1

AT's phenomenological models use abstract parameters (α, τd, τp) to demonstrate what selection looks like, but do not specify the mechanism by which selection operates in chemical systems. FM-ABM provides precisely this mechanism.

3. The Free-Market Agent-Based Model

3.1 Economic–Chemical Mapping

The FM-ABM maps chemistry onto a market economy with a direct correspondence between economic and chemical entities:

WorkersMarket agents (autonomous economic actors)Cells in assembly space

Ring neighborhoodsLocal market structure, O(k) circular topologyInteraction range

Witness producersState-owned enterprises preserving rare speciesMemory / historical contingency

A crucial distinction: workers are not atoms. Workers are economic agents that trade atoms and molecules. This separation of agency from substance allows the model to capture selection dynamics that cannot be modeled by physical simulations alone.

3.2 The Workshop Manager's Day

Each simulation step, every worker executes a five-phase economic cycle:

d (discovery) AT: α (selection) AT: decay AT: τp (production) AT: amplification

The five phases map directly onto AT's dynamical processes. Discovery controls τd. GFCF demand implements α. Producer management provides decay. Startup and production control τp. No phase is designed for AT — the mapping is emergent.

3.3 Physics Integration

FM-ABM grounds every reaction in physical chemistry through three layers of validation:

The Evans–Polanyi barrier introduces temperature as a tunable parameter. Different temperatures unlock different reaction pathways, providing a concrete mechanism for pathway selection that AT's abstract framework cannot capture.

4. FM-ABM as AT's Forward Dynamics

4.1 Implementing AT's Selection Parameter

AT defines α abstractly as “the specificity of selection — that some objects are more likely to be used to make new things.”1 This definition requires an external specification of which objects are preferred.

FM-ABM implements α concretely through Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) demand. Each worker demands the most complex molecule available in the local market. This creates a demand cascade: complex molecules are consumed, driving demand for their precursors, which drives demand for their precursors, down to primary atoms. The result is directed production (α < 1) without any hand-designed specification of which molecules should be preferred.

AT's approach: Set α = 0.5 (or some value < 1) in the production equation Pa ∝ (Na)α. Selection is put in by hand.
FM-ABM's approach: GFCF demand + market competition naturally produces α < 1 behavior. Selection emerges from economic dynamics.

4.2 Distinguishing Novelty from Noise

AT identifies the central problem: “physics has no functional view of the Universe, it cannot distinguish novel functional features from random fluctuations.”1

FM-ABM resolves this through two coupled mechanisms:

Exploration: Neighbor Interaction

When a worker's molecule encounters a neighbor's molecule and they combine exothermically (BDE check) into a geometrically valid product (UFF check), a genuinely novel product is created. This is not random fluctuation — it is thermodynamically grounded discovery. The product's existence is physics-validated.

Selection: Market Consumption

If the new product satisfies demand (GFCF wants it, or a producer needs it as input), it gets produced repeatedly. If not, it decays as its producer becomes unprofitable and closes. Market demand provides the functional evaluation that AT says physics lacks.

The combination is key: exploration proposes candidates (AT's τd), and market selection amplifies the functional ones (AT's α < 1). Neither mechanism alone suffices — just as AT predicts.

4.3 Objects Defined by Formation Histories

AT insists on treating “objects not as point particles, but as entities defined by their possible formation histories.”1 FM-ABM embodies this principle completely:

  • Every molecule in the census has a complete formation history — its recipe depth (assembly index), its input molecules, their input molecules, all the way back to primary atoms.
  • The recipe book is the assembly space — all discovered pathways for all observed objects.
  • Multiple independent routes to the same molecule are preserved (up to 260 routes for a single amino acid).
  • A molecule's “identity” includes not just its formula but the entire tree of operations that produced it.
Key correspondence: AT's assembly space is a theoretical construct — the set of all possible formation pathways. FM-ABM's recipe book is an empirical assembly space, progressively explored through thermodynamically grounded discovery. The recipe book grows over time exactly as AT predicts: “assembly contingent” (discovered pathways) expands within “assembly possible” (all valid combinations).

5. Experimental Validation

AT interpretation: The amino acids are thermodynamic attractors in assembly space. Their high copy number at high assembly index (recipe depth 3–6) is precisely what AT predicts as the hallmark of selection. FM-ABM shows that free-market dynamics are sufficient to generate this signature.

5.3 The Non-Monotonic Copy Signature

AT's central prediction is that selection produces non-monotonic copy-number distributions: at some assembly index a*, copy numbers increase despite rising complexity. This is impossible under undirected production, where the probability of producing an object at index a + 1 is always lower than at index a.

In our Budget = 1 simulation:

Avg copies5301072911729143826

Copies at depth 8 (avg = 38) exceed copies at depth 4 (avg = 11). This 3.5× amplification at higher assembly index is AT's “smoking gun” — the signature that selection must be operating.

AT's equation for copy-number dynamics:

dNa+1/dt  =  kd × (Na)α

produces non-monotonicity when α < 1 (directed selection). Our market achieves effective α < 1 through GFCF demand cascades — not hand-designed, but emergent from the economic dynamics of supply, demand, and competition.

Critical comparison: In AT's published models, α is set as an input parameter. In FM-ABM, the effective α emerges from market dynamics. The non-monotonic signature appears without any explicit instruction to produce it — it is a prediction of the model, not an assumption.

5.4 Temperature as a Pathway Selector

The Evans–Polanyi kinetic barrier (Ea = E0 + αEP × ΔE) makes temperature a tunable parameter that unlocks different reaction pathways. This provides a concrete physical mechanism for what AT calls “the specificity of selection.”

At T = 400 K (127 °C), a new 3-step synthesis route to histamine becomes accessible that was kinetically blocked at 300 K. This has a practical implication: “raise temperature to 127 °C to unlock a 3-step synthesis.” FM-ABM does not merely detect that different temperatures produce different chemistry — it predicts which pathways open at which temperatures.

Forecasting capability: AT can measure the assembly of a molecular ensemble after observation. FM-ABM can predict the assembly that will result from specific initial conditions (atoms, temperature, discovery budget) before observation. This is the difference between measurement and forecasting.

The relationship is complementary, not competitive. AT provides the theoretical framework for understanding what selection looks like in molecular systems. FM-ABM provides a computational laboratory for exploring how that selection operates and what it produces under specified conditions.

The key advance: AT demonstrates that high assembly + high copy number requires selection. FM-ABM demonstrates that free-market dynamics are sufficient to produce selection. Together, they establish that Darwinian economic optimization is a viable mechanism for the emergence of molecular complexity.

6.2 Broader Applications

The FM-ABM framework extends beyond prebiotic chemistry:

  • Prebiotic chemistry: A time machine for molecular evolution. Starting from bare atoms, FM-ABM generates the same amino acid hierarchy as nature — suggesting that the origin of life's building blocks is thermodynamically inevitable, not contingent.
  • Drug synthesis: Pathway discovery and optimization at different temperatures. The Evans–Polanyi barrier makes FM-ABM a practical tool for identifying novel synthesis routes and predicting the temperature at which they become accessible.
  • Macroeconomics: The same DEPLOYERS framework forecasts GDP and macroeconomic dynamics7. The mapping between chemistry and economics is not metaphorical — it is structural. Supply, demand, and competition operate identically in both domains.
  • Universal selection mechanism: The fact that the same computational framework (a) discovers amino acids from atoms, (b) reproduces AT's three selection regimes, and (c) forecasts macroeconomic variables suggests that free-market dynamics may be a fundamental mechanism for selection across domains. This aligns with AT's aspiration to identify “the physics of selection.”

6.3 Limitations

We acknowledge several limitations of the current FM-ABM implementation:

  • Structural isomers: FM-ABM operates on molecular formulas, correctly identifying compositions (e.g., C2H5NO2 for glycine) but not always resolving the correct 3D structure among isomers. UFF validation eliminates geometrically strained structures, but the resulting isomer may differ from the biologically relevant one.
  • Aromatic ring formation: The current bond-breaking/bond-forming logic does not support aromatic ring closure from intermolecular C=C bonds. This prevents discovery of phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan.
  • Kinetic barriers: Evans–Polanyi barriers are approximate. Full DFT transition-state calculations would provide more accurate kinetics but at 4–6 orders of magnitude higher computational cost9.
  • Isomer determination: The current approach serves as a starting point for ab-initio detailed determination of preferred isomers among those FM-ABM identifies as thermodynamically accessible.

7. Conclusions

We have demonstrated that a Free-Market Agent-Based Model provides an explicit, physics-grounded mechanism for the selection that Assembly Theory measures. The key findings are:

  1. FM-ABM is the first computational system providing a mechanism for AT's selection. The mechanism is Darwinian economic optimization: supply, demand, and competition among economic agents enacting chemistry.
  2. The mechanism operates on real chemistry. Bond Dissociation Energies, Universal Force Field geometry validation, and Evans–Polanyi kinetic barriers ground every reaction in physical chemistry.
  3. FM-ABM discovers the same amino acids as nature. 10 of 12 feasible amino acids emerge from bare atoms in minutes — the same “early 10” identified by Miller–Urey experiments, meteorite analysis, and phylogenetics.
  4. AT's three-regime prediction is confirmed. Stagnation, selection, and tar regimes emerge from a single tunable parameter (discoveryBudget), matching AT's τd/τp framework exactly.
  5. The non-monotonic copy signature emerges spontaneously. Market dynamics produce effective α < 1 without any explicit parameterization — selection is not put in by hand but arises from economic competition.
  6. FM-ABM forecasts, not just explains. Given initial conditions (atoms, temperature, discovery budget), FM-ABM predicts what molecules will be selected, which pathways will be discovered, and at what temperatures new routes become accessible.
In summary: Where Assembly Theory asks “has selection occurred?” (and answers by measuring assembly index + copy number), FM-ABM asks “what will selection produce?” (and answers by running the forward dynamics). Assembly Theory is the thermometer. FM-ABM is the weather model.

8.3 Evans–Polanyi Kinetic Barrier

Ea = E0 + αEP × ΔE       P = exp(−Ea / kT)

where E0 is the intrinsic barrier, αEP is the Brønsted coefficient (typically 0.3–0.5), and T is the simulation temperature in Kelvin. Reactions with P below a threshold are rejected.
