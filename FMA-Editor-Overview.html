Thesis

Darwinian free-market optimization is a universal mechanism for the emergence of complexity in open-ended systems. The same 18 mechanisms—birth/death dynamics, Evans-Polanyi activation barriers, persistent recipe books, census coverage, depth penalty, GFCF cascades, and others—produce qualitatively correct behaviour in five domains that share no substrate, no scale, and no evolutionary history: macroeconomic forecasting, prebiotic chemistry, language modelling, adaptive immunity, and robotic task planning. These are not analogies. They are structural isomorphisms governed by a single set of equations.

The Orchestration Principle

In every domain, FMA operates one level above the domain's native optimization. Gradient descent optimizes within an architecture; FMA optimizes the architecture itself. Individual transactions set prices; FMA decides which firms exist. Individual reactions follow thermodynamic gradients; FMA decides which molecular species persist. The same relationship holds across all five domains: the local optimizer handles individual interactions, while FMA handles the population dynamics of the agents performing those interactions.

Three Universal Regimes

Stagnation (low EP temperature): no selection, random exploration.
Selection (optimal): specialization, competition, emergent complexity.
Tar (high): runaway positive feedback, collapse of structure.

These three regimes appear identically across all domains. In economics: stasis, growth, bubble/crash. In chemistry: random reactions, life's toolkit, tar. In AI: static experts, monosemantic specialization, polysemantic churn. In immunology: tolerance, adaptive immunity, cytokine storm. A single parameter (Evans-Polanyi temperature) controls the regime boundary in every case.

Three Unique Selling Points

1. Genuine universality with mechanistic precision. Unlike previous universal frameworks (complexity theory, Assembly Theory), FMA provides a constructive algorithm with named, testable mechanisms. It does not describe complexity abstractly; it builds it from scratch.

2. Cross-domain falsifiability. Every mechanism makes quantitative predictions that can be tested independently in each domain. The six immunological predictions (e.g., exhaustion scales with cascade depth, not antigen dose) are novel and experimentally accessible.

3. Interactive reproducibility. Chapters 4 and 5 include browser-based interactive simulators (nanoFMT, nanoImmune) that let readers explore FMA dynamics in real time. The full HTML book, including all interactive viewers, is available at the accompanying online repository.

Competing and Complementary Works

The Origins of Order (Kauffman, 1993): self-organization in biology; FMA provides the constructive mechanism Kauffman describes abstractly. Scale (West, 2017): scaling laws across systems; FMA explains the population dynamics underlying those laws. Assembly Theory (Cronin & Walker, 2023): chemical complexity measure; FMA's regime transitions quantitatively match AT's predicted thresholds. The Economy as an Evolving Complex System (Arthur, 1997): evolutionary economics; FMA formalizes and extends the agent-based approach with universal mechanisms.
