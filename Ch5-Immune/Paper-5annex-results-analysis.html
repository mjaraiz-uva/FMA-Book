The immune system is a free market in which lymphocyte clones are firms, antigens are demand signals, and cytokines are prices.
1. The Key Insight: Orchestration Above Molecular Recognition

Classical immunology treats the adaptive immune system as a collection of molecular interactions: TCR binding, MHC presentation, cytokine signalling. These interactions are well-characterized, but they describe what happens at the molecular level. They do not explain who decides which clones expand, when expansion stops, and how memory is formed.

FMA provides the missing layer. Every named FMA mechanism has a precise immunological counterpart:

Key insight. The three FMA dynamical regimes—stagnation, selection, and tar—correspond precisely to immune tolerance (low inflammation, no clonal expansion), normal adaptive immunity (targeted expansion, contraction, memory), and cytokine storm (uncontrolled positive feedback overwhelming homeostasis). A single parameter (Evans-Polanyi temperature) controls all three, just as the inflammatory milieu controls the immune regime.
2. Panel-by-Panel Analysis of the nanoImmune Viewer

The viewer displays six real-time charts. Each panel corresponds to an FMA observable with a direct immunological interpretation.

1 Population Dynamics

What it shows: Total lymphocyte count (active + memory cells) over time.

FMA interpretation: This is the agent population—equivalent to firm count in the macroeconomic model. Birth events (clonal expansion) increase the population; death events (contraction, apoptosis) decrease it.

What to look for: In the Normal scenario, expect a sharp expansion peak followed by contraction to a slightly elevated baseline (memory pool). In Cytokine Storm, the population grows explosively without contraction. In Chronic, a sustained plateau reflects persistent antigenic stimulation preventing full contraction.

2 Birth/Death Ratio

What it shows: The ratio of clonal births to deaths, smoothed over a moving window. This is the primary regime indicator.

FMA interpretation: B/D > 1 indicates net population growth (selection regime); B/D < 1 indicates net decline (returning toward stagnation/homeostasis); B/D ≫ 1 indicates runaway expansion (tar regime = cytokine storm).

What to look for: The B/D ratio is the single most important diagnostic. In normal immunity, it spikes briefly above 1 then returns. In cytokine storm, it stays high. This confirms FMA Prediction 5: cytokine storm severity is predicted by birth/death ratio.

3 Affinity Maturation

What it shows: Average binding affinity of active lymphocyte clones over time.

FMA interpretation: This is the equivalent of firm profitability. Clones with higher affinity win more demand (antigen binding), expand more, and survive longer. Affinity maturation is the market's quality-improvement mechanism—unprofitable firms (low-affinity clones) die, profitable ones (high-affinity) expand.

What to look for: In the Normal scenario, affinity rises during the expansion phase as selection pressures improve the pool. In Vaccination scenarios, the second encounter starts at a higher baseline due to memory clones carrying pre-matured affinities.

4 Average Exhaustion (Depth Penalty)

What it shows: Mean exhaustion level across the active population.

FMA interpretation: The depth penalty—accumulated cost of deep processing cascades. In immunology: T-cell exhaustion from sustained antigen exposure. This is a key FMA prediction: exhaustion scales with cascade depth (number of sequential activation events), not with antigen dose per se.

What to look for: Chronic infection shows steadily rising exhaustion, eventually capping the immune response despite ongoing antigen presence. This is the hallmark of PD-1/LAG-3 upregulation in real chronic infections (HIV, HCV, tumours). The Normal scenario shows a brief exhaustion bump that resolves with contraction.

5 Recipe Book (Immunological Memory)

What it shows: Cumulative count of recipes (successful immune response chains) stored in the persistent memory.

FMA interpretation: The recipe book is FMA's equivalent of immunological memory—a permanent record of pathogen-specific response sequences. Each recipe encodes which clones responded, in what order, and with what effector profile.

What to look for: Recipes accumulate after successful responses (Normal, Vaccination). In the Vaccination scenario, the booster dose adds recipes on top of the primary response, creating a richer memory pool. In Cytokine Storm, few recipes are stored because the system never reaches controlled resolution.

6 Dynamical Regime Timeline

What it shows: A colour-coded strip indicating the current regime at each simulation step—yellow for stagnation, green for selection, red for tar.

FMA interpretation: The three universal regimes, visible in real time. The regime transitions are determined by the Evans-Polanyi temperature parameter (= inflammatory milieu).

What to look for: Normal immunity traces yellow→green→yellow (homeostasis, targeted response, return to homeostasis). Cytokine Storm traces yellow→green→red (fails to return). Chronic Infection stays in a flickering green zone. Lymphopenia stays yellow (insufficient population for selection).
