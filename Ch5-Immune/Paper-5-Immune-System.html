The adaptive immune system solves a problem that no central planner can address: defending an organism against an antigen space of approximately 1018 possible epitopes, using only local information, without knowing in advance which threats will appear. We show that every named mechanism of the Free-Market Algorithm (FMA)—a metaheuristic optimization framework independently validated in macroeconomic forecasting1 and prebiotic chemistry2—has a precise, mechanistically grounded counterpart in adaptive immunity. This correspondence is not metaphor: each of the 18 FMA mechanisms maps onto a specific immunological process, from the Evans–Polanyi activation barrier (lymphocyte activation thresholds) to the persistent recipe book (immunological memory), from census coverage (repertoire diversity maintenance) to the GFCF demand cascade (epitope spreading and cytokine amplification). The adaptive immune system did not read Hayek. It discovered the free market by natural selection, running the same algorithm that governs macroeconomic coordination and prebiotic molecular assembly.

1. Introduction

1.1 The Central Planning Problem in Immunology

The human adaptive immune system faces an optimization problem of staggering dimensionality. The antigen universe—the set of all molecular structures that could serve as foreign epitopes—spans approximately 1018 possible peptide-MHC configurations3. No germline genome could possibly encode a pre-specified receptor for each potential threat. The combinatorial space is too large, the threats too unpredictable, and the metabolic cost of maintaining a standing army of specialists for every possible antigen too great.

This is, in precise economic terms, a central planning problem. Hayek demonstrated in 1945 that no central authority can possess the distributed knowledge necessary for optimal resource allocation4. The immune system confronts the identical impossibility: no single gene, cell, or organ possesses the information needed to specify the required defensive repertoire. Instead, the system must discover specialists on demand, amplify those that prove useful, retire those that do not, and remember successful responses for future deployment—all using only local information, with no global loss function to optimize.

This is not a new observation in outline. Burnet's clonal selection theory (1957) recognized that lymphocytes are selected by antigen rather than instructed by it5. Jerne anticipated the idea even earlier, framing antibody formation as a natural-selection process6. What has been missing for nearly seventy years is a quantitative formalism—one that specifies the selection mechanism precisely enough to generate falsifiable predictions about phenomena that clonal selection theory alone does not address: the kinetics of activation, the depth-dependent dynamics of exhaustion, the conditions under which the system enters catastrophic cytokine storm, and the relationship between antigen complexity and memory quality.

1.2 What FMA Adds to Seventy Years of Clonal Selection Theory

The Free-Market Algorithm (FMA), introduced formally in Chapter 1 of this volume7, is a metaheuristic optimization framework based on market dynamics: autonomous agents discover transformation rules, trade goods, open and close firms, and compete for demand, with no centralized controller. Critically, FMA was designed and validated in two domains entirely unrelated to immunology—macroeconomic forecasting1 (MAE 0.42 percentage points for GDP prediction across 33 countries) and prebiotic chemistry2 (autonomous discovery of all 12 feasible amino acids, all 5 nucleobases, and the complete formose sugar chain from bare atoms)—before the immunological mapping described here was attempted.

FMA adds to clonal selection theory what Newton's laws added to Kepler's observations: a quantitative mechanism from which the observed phenomena can be derived rather than merely described. Where Burnet says "cells are selected," FMA specifies the Evans–Polanyi barrier that governs selection kinetics (PassesKineticBarrier() in the reference implementation7). Where immunologists observe "T-cell exhaustion," FMA derives it from a depth penalty that accumulates along the cascade chain (kDepthPenaltyKJ). Where clinicians treat cytokine storm as an inflammatory excess, FMA identifies it as a specific dynamical regime—the tar regime—in which the birth/death ratio of specialist producers exceeds a critical threshold.

2. Background

2.1 Clonal Selection and Expansion

The clonal selection theory, proposed by Burnet5 and anticipated by Jerne6, holds that each lymphocyte bears a unique receptor generated by stochastic V(D)J recombination. When antigen engages a receptor with sufficient affinity, the cell is activated, proliferates, and differentiates into effector cells. Cells whose receptors do not encounter their cognate antigen remain quiescent or undergo apoptosis. The result is a population of expanded clones precisely tailored to the threat at hand—specialist producers born in response to demand, in FMA terms.

2.2 Affinity Maturation and Germinal Centers

Following initial activation, B cells enter germinal centers (GCs) in secondary lymphoid organs, where they undergo somatic hypermutation (SHM) of their immunoglobulin genes and compete for antigen displayed on follicular dendritic cells (FDCs). B cells with improved affinity receive T follicular helper (Tfh) survival signals; those with reduced affinity undergo apoptosis. This iterative cycle of mutation, selection, and re-competition—typically lasting 2–3 weeks—drives a 100- to 10,000-fold increase in antibody binding affinity8. Victora and Nussenzweig (2012) provided the definitive characterization of this process, establishing that GC B cells cycle between dark zone (proliferation and mutation) and light zone (selection by antigen and Tfh cells).9

2.3 T-Cell Exhaustion

Chronic antigen stimulation—as occurs in persistent viral infections (HIV, HCV, HBV) and cancer—drives CD8+ T cells into a dysfunctional state termed exhaustion. Exhausted T cells progressively lose effector functions (cytokine production, cytotoxicity, proliferative capacity) and upregulate inhibitory receptors (PD-1, TIM-3, LAG-3, CTLA-4)10. Wherry (2011) demonstrated that exhaustion is not mere activation-induced cell death but a distinct differentiation program governed by the transcription factor TOX10. Critically, exhaustion severity correlates with the duration of antigen exposure, not its absolute magnitude—a point that will prove central to our FMA interpretation.

2.4 Cytokine Storm

Cytokine release syndrome (CRS), colloquially "cytokine storm," is a life-threatening systemic inflammatory response characterized by massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-1β) leading to multi-organ failure11. Fajgenbaum and June (2020) established the modern classification of CRS, distinguishing it from sepsis and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) and identifying the IL-6 amplification loop as a central mechanism11. CRS gained renewed attention during the COVID-19 pandemic, where it contributed significantly to mortality12, and in the context of CAR-T cell therapy, where it is the most common severe adverse event13.

2.5 Immunological Memory

Following antigen clearance, a fraction of expanded clones differentiates into long-lived memory B cells and memory T cells. Memory B cells can persist for decades and, upon re-exposure, mount a secondary response that is faster (days vs. weeks), of higher magnitude, and of higher affinity than the primary response. Long-lived plasma cells in bone marrow niches continuously secrete antibody without re-stimulation, providing constitutive protection14. The persistence and additivity of immunological memory—new responses do not overwrite old ones—will prove directly analogous to FMA's recipe book.

2.6 FMA Summary

The Free-Market Algorithm is specified fully in Chapter 17. We summarize only the features essential for the immunological mapping. FMA consists of a universal market engine (Layer 1) and pluggable domain rules (Layer 2). The market engine implements: (a) autonomous agents (MarketWorker) with inventory and production capability; (b) demand-driven specialist birth and idle-triggered death (WorkerTryStartup(), WorkerManageProducer()); (c) an Evans–Polanyi kinetic barrier gating new discoveries (PassesKineticBarrier()); (d) a depth penalty penalizing long synthesis chains (kDepthPenaltyKJ); (e) a persistent recipe book storing every successful transformation (RecipeBook); (f) census coverage ensuring every known product has at least one producer (CensusCoverage logic in ChooseProducerType()); (g) a GFCF demand cascade propagating demand for complex goods down the supply chain (ComputeGFCF logic in WorkerBuyGoods()); and (h) a witness mechanism amplifying demand when a new product is first discovered (SpawnWitnessProducer()). All agents execute discovery, trading, and production in the same loop at every timestep (WorkerMonthlyActivity()). The seven equations—agent cycle, Evans–Polanyi barrier, depth penalty, census coverage, witness boost, decay law, and GFCF cascade—are identical across all five domains described in this volume.

3.1 Evans–Polanyi Barrier: The Activation Threshold

In FMA, every discovery attempt must pass a kinetic barrier before it is accepted. The barrier height follows the Evans–Polanyi relation:

Ea = E0 + α · ΔE     (clamped to ≥ 0);    P(accept) = exp(−Ea / kT)

In the reference implementation (PassesKineticBarrier()), E0 is the base activation energy, α the Brønsted coefficient (default 0.3 for chemistry), and ΔE the thermodynamic driving force7. In the immune system, this maps directly onto the two-signal model of lymphocyte activation15. Signal 1 (TCR/BCR engagement with antigen) provides the thermodynamic driving force ΔE: higher binding affinity means more favorable ΔE. Signal 2 (co-stimulation via CD28–B7 interaction) modulates the baseline barrier E0: without co-stimulation, the barrier is insurmountable (anergy). The prediction α≈0.5—the same value observed in chemistry—is developed as Prediction P1 in Section 9.

3.2 Depth Penalty: Exhaustion Is Not Dose, It Is Depth

FMA imposes a depth penalty of 10 kJ/mol per synthesis step (kDepthPenaltyKJ = 10.0). This means that longer production chains are progressively less favorable, regardless of the thermodynamic quality of the final product. The immunological parallel is T-cell exhaustion10. Exhaustion correlates not with the total amount of antigen a T cell encounters, but with the duration and depth of chronic stimulation. Each round of antigen restimulation adds an increment of exhaustion—upregulation of PD-1, TIM-3, LAG-3, epigenetic remodeling via TOX—just as each synthesis step adds depth penalty in FMA. This reframing has a testable consequence: at constant total antigen dose, chronic low-level exposure should produce more exhaustion than acute high-dose exposure (Prediction P2).

3.3 Recipe Book: Immunological Memory Is Additive and Persistent

The FMA recipe book (RecipeBook) stores every successfully discovered transformation. Recipes are additive—new discoveries never overwrite existing ones—and depth-tracked, meaning the system knows the number of assembly steps required for each product. Immunological memory B and T cells are the biological recipe book. New immune responses generate new memory cells without erasing existing ones14. Memory is depth-tracked: affinity-matured memory B cells carry a record of their mutational history, and the quality of subsequent responses depends on the depth of prior affinity maturation. The recipe book's additivity explains why vaccination against a new pathogen does not compromise immunity to previously encountered pathogens—catastrophic forgetting is impossible by construction.

3.4 Census Coverage: Maintaining Repertoire Diversity

In FMA, when any discovered product has zero active producers, the census coverage mechanism (ChooseProducerType(), lines 839–853 of MarketSim.cpp) gives that product priority for new firm creation. This ensures that no discovered species vanishes entirely from the market. In the immune system, thymic selection and IL-7 homeostatic signaling serve the same function. IL-7 preferentially drives proliferation of T cells with low representation in the peripheral pool, preventing any single clone from dominating the naive repertoire16. This is not global optimization; it is local pressure toward diversity maintenance—precisely the census coverage mechanism.

3.5 Witness Mechanism: Dendritic Cells as Discovery Amplifiers

When a new product is discovered in FMA, the witness mechanism (SpawnWitnessProducer()) creates a sample unit and generates 3× amplified demand signals. This ensures that novel discoveries are not immediately lost to the market's inertia. Dendritic cells (DCs) are the biological witness: upon capturing a novel antigen, DCs migrate to lymph nodes and present processed peptides on MHC molecules while simultaneously releasing IL-12 and type I interferons—a massively amplified "demand signal" for specific T-cell clones17. The TLR/PAMP pattern-recognition system acts as the witness trigger: it detects novelty (molecular patterns associated with pathogens but absent from self) and amplifies the demand response accordingly.

3.6 GFCF Cascade: Demand Propagates Downward

FMA's GFCF (Gross Fixed Capital Formation) cascade, implemented in WorkerBuyGoods(), causes demand for the largest known product to propagate downward through the supply chain: demand for a complex molecule creates demand for its precursors, which creates demand for their precursors, all the way to primary goods. In the immune system, this manifests as epitope spreading and cytokine cascade amplification. The dominant B-cell response against an immunodominant epitope generates T-cell help that spills over to subdominant epitopes (epitope spreading). The IL-2 autocrine loop amplifies the signal: activated T cells secrete IL-2 that promotes their own expansion, creating a positive feedback cascade. The complement system provides a parallel amplification cascade: C3b opsonization marks antigen for enhanced uptake, generating more antigen presentation, which generates more T-cell activation—demand cascading downward through the supply chain.

4. The Training–Inference Collapse in Immunity

A central theme of this volume, first articulated in Chapter 4 (Free-Market Transformer), is the collapse of the training–inference dichotomy. In FMA, there is no separate training phase followed by a deployment phase. Every agent, at every timestep, executes both discovery and production in the same loop:

void MarketSim::WorkerMonthlyActivity(MarketWorker& worker) {
    if (m_uff) WorkerTryDiscovery(worker);   // "learning"
    WorkerBuyGoods(worker);                   // "trading"
    WorkerManageProducer(worker);             // "firm management"
    WorkerTryStartup(worker);                 // "entrepreneurship"
    WorkerRunProducer(worker);                // "production / inference"
    if (m_uff) WorkerDecayGoods(worker);      // "entropy"
}

Discovery (learning) and production (inference) are interleaved at the level of a single agent within a single timestep. There is no epoch structure, no forward-then-backward pass, no frozen-weight deployment. The same code path handles both.

4.1 The False Dichotomy in Immunology

Conventional immunology implicitly assumes a training–inference separation. The primary immune response is "training": naive lymphocytes encounter antigen for the first time, undergo clonal expansion and affinity maturation, and generate memory cells. The secondary response is "inference": memory cells recognize a returning pathogen and mount a rapid, high-affinity response using previously learned specificity. This framing suggests a clean temporal separation—first learn, then deploy.

This is false. The secondary immune response is not merely deployment of pre-learned responses:

  • Memory B cells undergo further affinity maturation during the secondary response. Recall memory B cells re-enter germinal centers, undergo additional rounds of SHM, and emerge with even higher affinity than their primary-response progenitors8,9.
  • Long-lived plasma cells continue to evolve in bone marrow niches. The bone marrow plasma cell compartment is not static; it undergoes ongoing selection and replacement14.
  • New epitopes on the same pathogen trigger new memory formation during "inference." A returning pathogen may present mutated or previously hidden epitopes, generating de novo memory alongside recall of existing memory.
  • T-cell memory is updated by rechallenge. Repeated encounters can either reinvigorate or exhaust memory T cells, depending on the duration and intensity of restimulation10.

In FMA terms, this is exactly what the code demands: WorkerTryDiscovery() and WorkerRunProducer() execute in the same loop. Every immune response is simultaneously learning (discovering new specificities, refining existing ones) and responding (deploying effector functions). The system does not switch modes.

4.2 Consequences of the Collapse

Why boosters work. Vaccine boosters are effective precisely because the secondary response is not pure inference. Each booster episode drives additional rounds of affinity maturation in germinal centers, progressively refining the recipe book. If secondary responses were pure inference—frozen-weight deployment—boosters would be unnecessary.
Original antigenic sin. First described by Francis (1960)18, original antigenic sin (OAS) is the phenomenon in which the immune response to a new variant of a previously encountered pathogen is dominated by memory cells from the original exposure, even when the new variant has diverged. In FMA terms, this is a direct consequence of recipe book persistence: early recipes, once stored, are consulted first. Subsequent variants encounter a recipe book already populated with prior solutions. Because recipes are additive and never overwritten, the original entries bias the response. OAS is not a bug—it is the cost of persistent memory without catastrophic forgetting.

Importantly, this simultaneous discovery-and-production architecture is not continual learning in the machine-learning sense. Continual learning struggles with catastrophic forgetting—new training overwrites old knowledge. FMA's recipe book is additive: new recipes never conflict with existing ones because each recipe occupies a unique niche in the production network. The immune system achieves the same property: memory cells for measles coexist indefinitely with memory cells for influenza, smallpox, and tetanus. The biological implementation of an additive recipe book is the long-lived memory cell compartment, where each clone occupies a distinct antigenic niche.

5. Three Dynamical Regimes

Chapter 1 established that FMA exhibits three dynamical regimes, governed by the ratio τd/τp (discovery rate to production rate)7. These same three regimes appear in immunity with striking clinical specificity.

Stagnation (τd/τp ≤ 0.3)

FMA: Discovery too slow relative to production. Market stagnates. Existing producers serve existing demand but nothing new emerges.

Immunity: Immune tolerance / anergy. Lymphocytes fail to activate despite antigen presence. Clonal anergy: T cells receive Signal 1 (TCR engagement) without Signal 2 (co-stimulation), driving them into a non-responsive state. Primary immunodeficiencies (SCID, CVID) represent the extreme: no discovery capacity at all.

Clinical: Immunodeficiency, persistent infection, failure to clear tumors.

Selection (τd/τp ≈ 0.5–1.0)

FMA: Discovery and production in balance. New recipes emerge; successful producers expand; unsuccessful ones close. The recipe book grows steadily.

Immunity: Normal adaptive immunity. Antigen-driven clonal expansion, affinity maturation, memory formation, and appropriate contraction. The immune system discovers, deploys, and remembers—the full cycle operates.

Clinical: Effective pathogen clearance, vaccine responsiveness, immune homeostasis.

Tar (τd/τp ≥ 2.0)

FMA: Discovery overwhelms production. Too many specialists born; not enough die. The market fills with redundant producers and runaway intermediates.

Immunity: Cytokine storm / autoimmunity. Uncontrolled clonal expansion without matching contraction. Pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ) amplify without negative feedback. Regulatory T cells and anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-β) are overwhelmed.

Clinical: COVID-19 CRS, septic shock, CAR-T toxicity, SLE, rheumatoid arthritis.

5.1 Cytokine Storm as Tar Regime: Birth/Death Ratio, Not Absolute Levels

Key reinterpretation. The conventional view of cytokine storm is that it represents "too much immunity"—excessive inflammatory cytokine production. FMA provides a more precise diagnosis: cytokine storm is failure of the producer death mechanism. The tar regime arises not from absolute production levels but from an imbalance in the birth/death ratio of specialist producers.

In FMA, producer death occurs when a firm has been idle for more than m_maxUnusedSteps (200 steps by default; WorkerManageProducer()). In the immune system, clonal contraction is mediated by anti-inflammatory signals: IL-10, TGF-β, regulatory T cells (Tregs), activation-induced cell death (AICD), and FAS/FASL interactions. Cytokine storm occurs when these death signals are overwhelmed or absent:

  • In COVID-19 CRS: SARS-CoV-2 directly impairs Treg function and drives sustained macrophage activation12
  • In CAR-T toxicity: engineered T cells lack normal contraction signals; they continue expanding against persistent antigen13
  • In hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH): defective perforin/granzyme pathways prevent target cell killing, maintaining persistent antigen stimulation
  • In autoimmunity (SLE, RA): failure of peripheral tolerance (defective Treg suppression) removes the death signal for self-reactive clones

In every case, the pathology is the same in FMA terms: producer birth rate exceeds producer death rate, the market fills with expanding producers that cannot be cleared, and the resulting oversupply of inflammatory mediators cascades through the GFCF mechanism into multi-organ damage.

Clinical implication. This analysis explains why early IL-6 receptor blockade (Tocilizumab) is more effective than late intervention in COVID-19 CRS19. Early blockade interrupts the birth signal amplification loop before the birth/death ratio becomes self-sustaining. Late intervention attempts to mop up downstream cytokines while the ratio remains imbalanced—the market is still overproducing. FMA predicts that therapeutic strategies targeting the birth/death ratio (simultaneous IL-6 blockade + Treg expansion, or combined checkpoint agonism) will outperform single-cytokine suppression.

6. Superposition in Immunity

Chapter 4 of this volume establishes that when the number of features F exceeds the number of experts N in a Free-Market Transformer, superposition arises: each expert is forced to encode multiple features, producing polysemanticity20. The immune system exhibits a precise parallel.

6.1 Normal Operation: Monosemanticity

Under normal conditions, each lymphocyte clone is monospecific. A naive B cell bears a unique BCR that recognizes one epitope (or a narrow family of structurally related epitopes). A naive T cell bears a unique TCR that recognizes one peptide-MHC complex. This is monosemanticity by construction—each "expert" in the immune market serves one "feature" (antigen). There is no compression: the system maintains approximately 107–108 distinct T-cell clones, each mono-specific3.

6.2 HIV/AIDS as Central Planning Failure

HIV targets CD4+ T helper cells—the coordination layer of the immune market. T helper cells do not kill pathogens directly; they provide cytokine "demand signals" that direct B cells to produce antibodies and CD8+ T cells to kill infected targets. HIV destroys the price system itself. Without T helper signals, B cells cannot receive demand signals for antibody production, and CD8+ T cells cannot receive the co-stimulatory support needed for full effector differentiation. The immune market loses its price coordination mechanism—not just workers, but the entire price signaling infrastructure.

6.3 Lymphopenia-Induced Superposition

When CD4+ T cell counts fall below critical thresholds (lymphopenia), the immune system triggers lymphopenia-induced proliferation (LIP): remaining T cells expand to fill the depleted niche. But this expansion is not selective—it is homeostatic, driven by IL-7 and self-peptide/MHC interactions16. The result is oligoclonal expansion: a small number of clones expand massively, each forced to respond to multiple antigens it was not originally specific for. This is superposition: too few experts (N↓), unchanged feature space (F fixed), forcing each expert to encode multiple features.

The clinical consequence is immune collapse—not from lack of cells (after LIP, total cell counts may partially recover) but from loss of monosemanticity. An oligoclonally expanded T-cell pool is like an over-compressed neural network: it responds to many stimuli but with poor specificity, failing to mount effective targeted responses against any single pathogen.

FMA prediction. IL-7 therapy in lymphopenia should be designed to preferentially expand rare clones (census coverage) rather than simply restoring total cell count. Simply pumping up cell numbers without restoring diversity amounts to further compressing the expert pool—oligoclonal expansion is still superposition. This generates Prediction P3 in Section 9.

7. The Germinal Center as a Local Free Market

The germinal center is perhaps the clearest biological instantiation of FMA's ring neighborhood mechanism. Consider the parallels in detail.

Figure 2 | The germinal center as a local free market. B cells cycle between the dark zone (proliferation and somatic hypermutation = random exploration) and the light zone (selection by antigen on FDCs and T follicular helper survival signals = market competition). Each GC is an independent local market with O(k) locality—no global optimization.

In each germinal center, B cells compete for a local resource: antigen displayed on FDCs. Only B cells whose mutated BCR achieves sufficient affinity for the displayed antigen (above the Evans–Polanyi threshold) receive Tfh cell survival signals. Those below the threshold undergo apoptosis—producer death. Surviving B cells return to the dark zone for another round of SHM (random exploration) and then re-compete. This cycle repeats for approximately 2–3 weeks, driving a progressive increase in average antibody affinity9.

Critically, this is not global optimization. Each germinal center is an independent local market. Multiple GCs in different lymph nodes can operate simultaneously, each converging on a local optimum for its particular antigen. B cells in one GC do not compete with B cells in another GC for the same antigen. This is the RingNeighborhood mechanism: O(k) locality, where k is the number of B cells within a single GC.

The contrast with neural networks is instructive. In standard deep learning, gradients are pooled globally: every training example contributes to updates across the entire parameter space. In the germinal center, selection is purely local. Two antigens presented in the same GC do not synergize—they compete for the same limited Tfh help, potentially impairing affinity maturation for both. This generates Prediction P6: co-localized antigens should interfere with each other's affinity maturation, not improve it.

8. Assembly Theory Connection

Assembly Theory (AT), formalized by Sharma et al.21, provides an intrinsic measure of complexity: the assembly index of an object is the minimum number of recursive joining operations needed to construct it from basic building blocks. Objects with both high assembly index and high copy number are, AT argues, the signature of selection. Chapter 1 established that FMA provides the constructive mechanism that AT describes: where AT measures, FMA generates7.

8.2 The τd/τp Ratio in Germinal Center Dynamics

The two FMA timescales map quantitatively onto GC dynamics:

τd = SHM rate × GC cycle time ≈ 10−4 mutations/bp/division × 6 h/cycle
τp = clonal expansion doubling time ≈ 8–12 h
τd/τp ≈ 0.01–0.1 → maps to the selection regime

This ratio places normal germinal center dynamics squarely in the selection regime of the AT phase diagram—precisely where productive affinity maturation occurs. When the ratio shifts, pathology follows:

  • AID overexpression (activation-induced cytidine deaminase, the enzyme that drives SHM) increases τd, pushing the ratio upward toward the tar regime. AID overexpression is the leading risk factor for B-cell lymphomas22—consistent with tar regime prediction: uncontrolled "discovery" (mutation) overwhelms selection, producing runaway clonal expansion.
  • AID deficiency (hyper-IgM syndrome) reduces τd to near zero, trapping the system in the stagnation regime: no affinity maturation, no class switching, persistent low-affinity IgM responses.
Assembly Theory in three sentences. Naive BCRs are low-assembly-index objects: simple joins of germline gene segments. Affinity maturation increases their assembly index through selection-guided mutation. The copy number of high-index antibodies (109–1012 identical molecules from each plasma cell clone) provides the high-copy-number / high-assembly-index signature that AT identifies as the universal hallmark of selection. The germinal center is a biological assembly engine.

9. Six Novel Predictions

The FMA formalism, applied to immunity, generates six falsifiable predictions. Each is specified with hypothesis, experimental protocol, expected measurement, and falsification criterion. None of these predictions follows from clonal selection theory alone; each requires the quantitative FMA framework.

P1: Evans–Polanyi T-Cell Activation Kinetics (α ≈ 0.5)

Hypothesis. T-cell activation probability follows the Evans–Polanyi relation: P(activation) ∝ exp(−Ea/kT), where Ea = E0 + α · |ΔG(TCR–pMHC)|, with α ≈ 0.5—the same Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi coefficient observed in chemical catalysis2.

Experimental setup. Vary TCR–pMHC binding affinity (Kd) systematically using altered peptide ligands (APLs) across a panel of well-characterized TCR–pMHC pairs. For each APL, measure the activation threshold (IL-2 production, CD69 upregulation) as a function of CD28 co-stimulation strength (which modulates E0).

Expected result. A plot of ln(Pactivation) vs. ΔGbinding yields a linear relationship with slope α ≈ 0.5 ± 0.15, across multiple TCR–pMHC systems. Varying co-stimulation shifts the intercept (E0) but not the slope (α).

Falsification. If α deviates from 0.5 by more than 0.2 across multiple TCR systems, or if the relationship is non-linear, the Evans–Polanyi model for T-cell activation is rejected.

P2: Exhaustion Scales with Cascade Depth, Not Dose

Hypothesis. T-cell exhaustion severity (PD-1, TIM-3, TOX expression) scales with the depth of antigen cascade (number of sequential restimulation rounds), not total antigen dose.

Experimental setup. Deliver the same total antigen dose to CD8+ T cells in two conditions: (a) a single high-dose pulse (depth = 1), and (b) chronic low-level exposure over many sequential rounds (depth = n). Match total integrated antigen exposure exactly.

Expected result. Chronic multi-round exposure produces significantly more exhaustion (higher PD-1/TIM-3/TOX expression, reduced cytokine production, reduced proliferative capacity) than equivalent-dose acute exposure. The exhaustion increment per restimulation round is approximately constant (additive depth penalty).

Falsification. If exhaustion correlates with total dose rather than number of restimulation rounds at constant total dose, the depth-penalty model is rejected.

P3: IL-7 Preferentially Expands Rare Clones (Census Coverage)

Hypothesis. IL-7 homeostatic signals preferentially drive expansion of clones with low current representation in the peripheral pool, implementing census coverage.

Experimental setup. Reconstitute lymphopenic mice with a defined TCR repertoire (known frequencies for each clone). Administer IL-7 therapy and measure clonal dynamics by deep TCR sequencing at intervals.

Expected result. Rare clones expand preferentially under IL-7, restoring a more uniform clonal distribution. The expansion rate of each clone is inversely proportional to its baseline frequency.

Falsification. If IL-7 expands all clones proportionally to their initial frequency (rich-get-richer), or if the effect is random with respect to initial frequency, the census coverage prediction is rejected.

P4: Memory Quality Scales with Antigen Assembly Index

Hypothesis. The quality of immunological memory (affinity, breadth, durability) scales with the structural complexity of the immunizing antigen, measured by its assembly index.

Experimental setup. Immunize cohorts with antigens of varying complexity: single synthetic peptide (low assembly index) → recombinant protein → virus-like particle (VLP) → attenuated whole pathogen (high assembly index). Measure memory B-cell affinity and breadth by single-cell BCR sequencing and functional assays at 6 months post-immunization.

Expected result. Memory response quality (average affinity, number of distinct epitopes targeted, durability of serum antibody titers) increases monotonically with antigen assembly index. Whole-pathogen immunization produces deeper, broader, more durable memory than peptide immunization.

Falsification. If memory quality is independent of antigen complexity (e.g., a single peptide generates memory equal to whole-pathogen immunization), the depth-tracking prediction is rejected.

P5: Cytokine Storm Severity Predicted by Birth/Death Ratio

Hypothesis. Cytokine storm severity is better predicted by the ratio of clonal expansion signals to clonal contraction signals than by absolute cytokine levels.

Experimental setup. In a COVID-19 patient cohort, measure at admission: (a) birth signals: IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ, absolute lymphocyte proliferation rate (Ki-67+ frequency); (b) death signals: IL-10, TGF-β, Treg frequency (CD4+CD25hiFoxP3+). Compute birth/death ratio = (IL-6 + TNF + proliferation rate) / (IL-10 + TGF-β + Treg%).

Expected result. Birth/death ratio at admission predicts progression to severe CRS (ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, death) with higher AUC-ROC than IL-6 alone or any single cytokine.

Falsification. If absolute IL-6 level at admission predicts CRS severity with AUC ≥ birth/death ratio, the ratio-based model offers no improvement and the FMA reinterpretation is not supported.

P6: GC Locality—Two Antigens Impair Mutual Affinity Maturation

Hypothesis. Introducing two distinct antigens into the same germinal center impairs affinity maturation for both, compared to single-antigen GCs.

Experimental setup. Use GC-targeted antigen delivery (e.g., α-DEC-205 conjugation) to deliver: (a) antigen A alone to one lymph node, (b) antigen B alone to another lymph node, and (c) both antigens A and B to a single lymph node. Measure affinity maturation kinetics for both antigens by serial serum sampling and single-GC microdissection with BCR sequencing.

Expected result. Co-localized antigens produce lower final affinity for both antigens compared to single-antigen controls. The effect is due to competition for limited Tfh help within the GC (local market saturation).

Falsification. If co-localized antigens show equal or improved affinity maturation compared to single-antigen controls, the O(k) locality prediction is rejected.

10. Cancer Immunotherapy as FMA Engineering

The FMA framework provides a unifying lens for the major modalities of cancer immunotherapy. At its core, cancer represents a tar regime failure at the cellular level: the tumour has escaped the normal birth/death balance that governs tissue homeostasis. But cancer does not merely evade the immune system passively—it actively engineers the local market to suppress immune dynamics.

10.1 The Tumour Microenvironment as Market Suppression

The tumour microenvironment (TME) systematically distorts FMA dynamics through two complementary strategies:

  • Suppressing producer death. Tumours upregulate PD-L1 (engaging PD-1 on T cells to induce exhaustion), CTLA-4 ligands, IDO (tryptophan depletion), and TGF-β (Treg induction). Each of these removes the signal that tells anti-tumour T cells to remain active—or activates the signal that tells them to stop. In FMA terms, the tumour raises the inactivity threshold beyond what anti-tumour producers can sustain, forcing premature producer death.
  • Amplifying tumour "producer birth." Tumours secrete VEGF (angiogenesis for nutrient supply), IL-6 (autocrine growth signaling), and EGF (proliferation signals). These amplify the tumour's own growth rate while simultaneously suppressing immune cell function—tilting the birth/death ratio in favor of the tumour at the expense of anti-tumour immunity.

10.2 Immunotherapy Modalities as FMA Mechanism Restoration

Each major immunotherapy modality can be understood as restoring a specific FMA mechanism that the tumour has disabled:

10.3 FMA Prediction for Combination Therapy

Prediction. Combination therapy synergy should be strongest between therapies that target both birth and death arms of the FMA cycle simultaneously. Specifically: checkpoint blockade (restoring producer birth by lowering activation barriers) combined with Treg depletion or IL-10 blockade (restoring producer death for tumour cells by removing immune suppression) should be more effective than dual checkpoint blockade (which targets only the birth arm).

The reasoning follows directly from the tar regime analysis. Cytokine storm risk in immunotherapy is determined by the birth/death ratio, not by individual pathway activation. Dual checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-1 + anti-CTLA-4) amplifies the birth signal on two parallel pathways but does not address the death arm—explaining why combination checkpoint blockade carries significantly higher autoimmune toxicity23. A birth+death combination (e.g., anti-PD-1 + anti-IL-10 or anti-PD-1 + Treg modulation) should achieve therapeutic efficacy while better controlling the birth/death ratio, reducing cytokine storm risk.

This prediction is consistent with emerging clinical evidence that next-generation immunotherapy combinations incorporating immune modulators (anti-LAG-3, anti-TIGIT) alongside checkpoint blockade show improved efficacy-to-toxicity ratios compared to dual checkpoint blockade23. The FMA framework provides a principled basis for designing such combinations: identify which arm of the birth/death ratio each therapy targets, and combine across arms rather than within a single arm.

11. Discussion

11.1 FMA as Universal Biology: Five Domains, One Algorithm

With this chapter, the FMA framework has been applied to five fundamentally different domains: macroeconomic forecasting1, prebiotic chemistry2, synthesis planning2, language modelling20, and adaptive immunity. In every case, the identical Layer 1 market engine—ring neighborhoods, demand propagation, firm entry/exit, trading, decay—produced self-organized production networks. Three structural properties re-appeared in every domain without being engineered into the domain rules:

  • Three dynamical regimes. Stagnation, selection, and tar emerge from the τd/τp ratio. In economics: recession, growth, hyperinflation. In chemistry: no discovery, optimal synthesis, runaway byproducts. In language modelling: mode collapse, generative diversity, expert fragmentation. In immunity: tolerance, normal adaptive immunity, cytokine storm.
  • Training–inference collapse. In every domain, discovery ("learning") and production ("deployment") execute in the same agent loop. The recipe book grows during operation. This is not continual learning—recipes are additive and never conflict, making catastrophic forgetting impossible by construction.
  • Monosemanticity by construction. Each specialist is born to serve a specific demand signal. Superposition arises only when the market is starved of workers—the analogue of polysemantic neurons in overloaded networks and oligoclonal collapse in lymphopenia.

The universality argument is strengthened by the fact that FMA was designed and validated in economics and chemistry before the immune system comparison was made. The five-domain convergence is a discovery, not a design choice.

11.2 What Immunity Teaches FMA

The mapping is not merely one-directional. Several features of the immune system suggest improvements to the FMA algorithm:

  • Cytokine-inspired adaptive rates. The immune system modulates its "temperature" dynamically through cytokine concentrations. Pro-inflammatory cytokines increase the effective τd/τp ratio (more aggressive discovery), while anti-inflammatory cytokines decrease it (consolidation). FMA could implement analogous adaptive temperature scheduling driven by market state—raising discovery aggressiveness when the market stagnates, damping it when signs of tar emerge.
  • GC-inspired local expert competition. The germinal center's dark zone / light zone cycling (mutation then selection, repeated iteratively) suggests a modification to FMA's discovery mechanism: instead of single-shot discovery attempts, create local "incubator" zones where multiple variants of a new product compete before entering the broader market.
  • Class switching as role reassignment. Immunoglobulin class switching (IgM → IgG → IgA → IgE) allows the same antigen-binding specificity to serve different effector functions. This suggests a mechanism whereby FMA producers could change their "output type" while retaining their core recipe—a form of specialization that the current implementation does not support.

11.3 Prior Immune Network Theories

The idea that the immune system operates as a network or market is not entirely new, but prior formulations lacked the quantitative specificity that FMA provides:

  • Jerne's immune network theory (1974)24 proposed that antibodies form a network of idiotype–anti-idiotype interactions, creating internal regulation. The theory was largely qualitative and did not generate testable quantitative predictions. FMA replaces the idiotypic network with explicit market dynamics—demand propagation, firm entry/exit, census coverage—and generates specific numerical predictions (α≈0.5, depth-dependent exhaustion).
  • Perelson's shape space model (1979)25 introduced a geometric framework for antigen–receptor binding. It provided a mathematical basis for repertoire coverage but did not include birth/death dynamics, memory, or regime transitions. FMA adds all three, plus the demand cascade mechanism that Perelson's static geometry does not capture.
  • Artificial immune systems (AIS)—Farmer et al. (1986)26, Hofmeyr and Forrest (2000)27—took inspiration from immunology to build computational algorithms for anomaly detection and optimization. This is the reverse direction: biology inspiring computation. FMA moves in the other direction: a computational framework developed for economics and chemistry is discovered to describe immunology, constituting an independent validation rather than a circular application.

What FMA adds to all prior approaches: (1) quantitative formalism with measurable parameters (E0, α, depth penalty, decay rate); (2) cross-domain validation before the immunological application; (3) the training–inference collapse, not recognized in any prior immune network theory; (4) the tar regime interpretation of cytokine storm as birth/death imbalance; and (5) six specific falsifiable predictions that go beyond conceptual mapping.

11.4 Limitations and Open Questions

We identify four limitations of the current analysis:

  1. Character-level vs. molecular-level biology. FMA operates at the level of species (molecular formulas or cell types), not at the level of individual molecular conformations or stochastic gene expression. The mapping captures population-level dynamics accurately but does not address single-cell stochasticity.
  2. Parameter estimation. While we predict α≈0.5 based on the chemistry analogy, the precise values of E0, the depth penalty per restimulation round, and the decay rate constants in the immune system remain to be measured experimentally. The predictions in Section 9 are designed to enable these measurements.
  3. Innate immunity. This chapter focuses on adaptive immunity. The mapping of FMA's Target mode to innate immunity (fixed PRRs, no memory) is noted in Table 1 but not developed in detail. The interplay between innate and adaptive immunity—particularly the role of innate signals in setting E0 (the co-stimulation requirement)—deserves a separate treatment.
  4. Spatial organization. The current FMA implementation uses a ring neighborhood topology, which captures the locality of GC interactions but does not reproduce the spatial anatomy of lymph nodes, spleen, and mucosal tissues. A spatially extended FMA with tissue-specific neighborhoods is a natural next step.

12. Conclusion

The adaptive immune system did not read Hayek. It discovered the free market by natural selection, running the same algorithm that governs macroeconomic coordination and prebiotic molecular assembly. The seven equations of Chapter 1—the agent cycle, Evans–Polanyi barrier, depth penalty, census coverage, witness boost, decay law, and GFCF cascade—unmodified, describe clonal selection, lymphocyte activation kinetics, T-cell exhaustion, repertoire diversity maintenance, dendritic cell amplification, homeostatic cell turnover, and cytokine cascade amplification as instances of one dynamical system.

The evidence is structural: all 18 named FMA mechanisms have precise immunological counterparts (Table 1), each citing a specific function in the reference implementation. It is existential: FMA was validated in economics and chemistry before this comparison, so the mapping is a discovery, not a post-hoc fit. And it is predictive: six novel falsifiable predictions follow from the formalism, including Evans–Polanyi activation kinetics, depth-dependent exhaustion, census coverage in IL-7 therapy, and cytokine storm as a birth/death ratio phenomenon.

The reinterpretation of cytokine storm as a tar regime—failure of producer death, not excess of producer birth—has immediate clinical implications. The extension to cancer immunotherapy shows that checkpoint blockade, CAR-T therapy, cancer vaccines, and adoptive cell transfer can each be understood as restoring a specific FMA mechanism that the tumour microenvironment has disabled. The framework provides a principled basis for combination therapy design: combine across birth and death arms of the FMA cycle, not within a single arm.

Most broadly, the immune system provides the strongest evidence yet for this volume's central thesis: Darwinian free-market optimization is a universal mechanism for the emergence of complexity in open-ended systems. That the same seven equations—written to describe molecular assembly in a simulation of prebiotic chemistry—should describe, without modification, the most sophisticated defence system in biology is either a remarkable coincidence or an indication that free-market dynamics occupy a deeper place in the organization of nature than we have recognized.

Closing thought. Hayek argued that the price system is a mechanism for aggregating distributed knowledge that no central planner could collect4. The immune system proves him right at the molecular level. Cytokines are prices. Lymphocytes are firms. The recipe book is memory. The germinal center is a local marketplace. And cytokine storm—the most feared complication in modern immunology—is what happens when the market fails: not too much commerce, but the collapse of the mechanism that clears bankrupt firms. Understanding immunity as a free market does not diminish its biology. It reveals the computation that biology is performing.
