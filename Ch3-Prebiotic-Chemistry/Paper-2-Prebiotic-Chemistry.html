Chapter 3 — Prebiotic Chemistry

Prebiotic Chemistry from a Free-Market Agent-Based Model: Emergence of Life's Initial Molecular Toolkit

Martin Jaraiz — Department of Electricity and Electronics, University of Valladolid, Spain
March 2026

Abstract. We present a computational "time machine" that replays prebiotic chemistry: starting from bare C, H, O, and N atoms, a free-market agent-based model (FM-ABM) grounded in physics — Bond Dissociation Energies (BDE) and Universal Force Field (UFF) strain evaluation — discovers thermodynamically favorable reaction pathways and spontaneously recovers key precursors of life's molecular toolkit: all 12 feasible amino acid formulas, all 5 canonical nucleobase formulas, the formose sugar chain from formaldehyde to ribose, Krebs cycle intermediates (pyruvate, succinate, malate), and the first tripeptide (Gly-Gly-Gly). Amino acid formulas emerge 14× earlier than random chance would predict, consistent with the thermodynamic inevitability hypothesis of Higgs & Pudritz (2009). The simulation runs on a standard laptop in under 5 minutes, discovering up to 260 independent synthesis routes for a single amino acid. We demonstrate the framework's practical utility by analyzing 8 independent synthesis routes to histamine, with full energy diagrams, supply chain trees, and multi-criteria ranking — a capability directly applicable to modern synthesis pathway design. Temperature-dependent analysis reveals that higher temperatures (400–600 K) unlock deeper reaction pathways, with depth-3 routes becoming accessible at 400 K. Finally, we show that the model's behavior quantitatively validates Assembly Theory predictions: three distinct regimes (stagnation, selection, tar) emerge from varying a single parameter, and non-monotonic copy-number distributions at high assembly index provide an explicit mechanism for AT's selection signature. No target molecules are specified; all products emerge purely from thermodynamic drive.

1. Compressing the Chemical Clock

In H.G. Wells' The Time Machine (1895), the protagonist builds a device to witness evolution unfold across millennia. We propose an analogous device for chemistry: imagine peering into a virtual micelle — a tiny compartment filled with C, H, O, and N atoms — and pressing fast-forward through hundreds of millions of years. Like a time-lapse movie, you watch atoms collide, bond, and build increasingly complex molecules until, in just minutes of simulation time, the building blocks of life emerge from the primordial soup.

The question of how amino acids arose on prebiotic Earth has been studied through four independent approaches:

Laboratory experiments

Miller & Urey (1953) passed electric sparks through CH4/NH3/H2O/H2 and found amino acids after days. Simple, but slow and hard to instrument at the molecular level.

Meteorite analysis

The Murchison meteorite (1969) contains the same amino acids, confirming abiotic synthesis occurs in space — no biology required.

Quantum chemistry

DFT and ab initio methods compute individual reaction pathways at supercomputer cost. Weeks per pathway; exploring the full combinatorial space is impractical.

Thermodynamic theory

Higgs & Pudritz (2009) showed that free energy rankings predict amino acid abundance. Elegant, but reveals products, not pathways.

We introduce a fundamentally different approach: a Darwinian Agent-Based Model (FM-ABM) implementation of a Free Market economic system, where chemistry is enacted by virtual workers — think of tiny dwarfs or daemons — who run workshops, trade molecules with their neighbors, and discover new reactions by trial and error. Each workshop transforms input molecules into products, guided only by energy: if the product is more stable (lower energy), the reaction succeeds. Supply and demand drive which workshops thrive. The system naturally discovers thermodynamically favorable pathways — and, remarkably, converges on the same amino acids, nucleobases, sugars, and metabolic intermediates that nature selected as the molecular toolkit of life.

This paper presents three interrelated results: (i) the spontaneous emergence of life's molecular toolkit from bare atoms, (ii) practical synthesis route discovery and multi-criteria ranking for target molecules such as histamine, and (iii) quantitative validation of Assembly Theory predictions through the explicit selection mechanism provided by the free-market dynamics.

2.2 Energy Evaluation

A reaction proceeds only if ΔE < 0 (exothermic):

ΔE = Estrain(product) − BDE(new bond)
where BDE is the Bond Dissociation Energy (e.g., C–C = 346, C=O = 799, C=N = 615 kJ/mol) and Estrain is the Universal Force Field (UFF) strain energy from RDKit 3D geometry optimization.

For molecules larger than 30 atoms, UFF noise exceeds the signal, so we use BDE only — or evaluate a local fragment (atoms within 2 bonds of the reaction site, ~8 atoms) for meaningful local strain discrimination.

This energy criterion is a deliberate coarse-graining of the full DFT-based microkinetic methodology developed in the author's prior computational chemistry work [Jaraiz, 2017; Jaraiz, 2019; Jaraiz, 2020b]. In that methodology, DFT-computed activation energies and reaction enthalpies serve as inputs to microkinetic simulators that model reaction rates and selectivities. Here, the same Evans–Polanyi linear free-energy relationship (Section 2.5) is retained, but individual DFT calculations are replaced by BDE tabulations and UFF strain energies — a justified approximation for the combinatorial exploration of thousands of candidate reactions under prebiotic conditions. The goal is not to compute individual pathway rates to DFT accuracy, but to identify which molecular formulas lie in thermodynamic energy minima and are therefore robust candidates for prebiotic synthesis. Selected pathways can subsequently be validated at the DFT level (Section 5).

2.4 The Workshop Manager's Day

Each simulation step, every agent manages its workshop and acts as a consumer. As consumers, workers generate demand via GFCF (Gross Fixed Capital Formation — borrowed from economics, it represents the drive to acquire increasingly complex goods, here molecules). As workshop managers, they handle production using only intermediate consumption (IC — input molecules), with no need for labor or capital:

  1. Discover — Try combining an inventory molecule with a neighbor's molecule. If ΔE < 0, a new recipe is born and the workshop can produce it.
  2. Buy goods (GFCF) — As consumers, workers prefer larger, more complex molecules (with some idiosyncratic variability), generating demand that propagates through the supply chain.
  3. Manage workshop — Close unprofitable workshops; protect the last producer of each product. These protected "essential-service" producers are likely only needed in our simulations to compensate for the small number of atoms simulated compared to real-world chemistry, where the sheer scale ensures that every feasible reaction is continuously re-enacted somewhere. In economic terms, they are analogous to state-owned enterprises — subsidized firms that provide services not covered by the private sector, stabilizing the system.
  4. Try startup — Open a new workshop if idle and demand exists.
  5. Run workshop — Produce using known recipes from IC inputs. Try to grow by combining the product with a neighbor's goods.

2.5 Kinetic Barriers

Thermodynamic equilibrium with kinetic filtering. The model focuses on thermodynamic favourability (ΔE < 0) but includes an Evans-Polanyi kinetic barrier:
Ea = E0 + α·ΔE     (E0 = 50 kJ/mol, α = 0.3)
with Boltzmann acceptance probability P = exp(−Ea/kT). Strongly exothermic reactions (ΔE < −167 kJ/mol) always proceed; weakly exothermic reactions are probabilistically suppressed. At temperature T, only reactions whose activation energy is comparable to kT have significant probability of occurring. This is justified by the geological time scales of prebiotic chemistry — hundreds of millions of years where thermodynamics dictates the destination and kinetics only affects the route.

The Evans-Polanyi framework introduces a physically meaningful temperature dependence: at low T (e.g., 288 K), only the most exothermic reactions proceed reliably; at higher T (e.g., 400–600 K), weakly exothermic reactions become accessible, opening deeper synthesis pathways. This temperature effect is explored quantitatively in Section 3.7.

2.6 Scope and Limitations

What this model is: A thermodynamic explorer that identifies which molecular formulas occupy energy minima in C,H,O,N combinatorial space and constructs plausible reaction trees connecting them. It is a first-pass filter — analogous to high-throughput virtual screening in drug discovery — that narrows the search space before expensive quantum chemical validation.

Several important caveats govern interpretation of the results:

  • Formula vs. stereoisomer identity. The model works with molecular formulas (SMILES connectivity graphs) and does not distinguish stereoisomers. "Glycine found at step 30" means the formula C2H5NO2 was reached via an exothermic pathway — the 3D stereochemistry is not resolved by this model.
  • BDE accuracy. Tabulated BDE values carry typical uncertainties of ±10–20 kJ/mol. This is sufficient to classify strongly exothermic reactions reliably, but not to rank competing pathways differing by <20 kJ/mol. The Evans-Polanyi kinetic filter mitigates this by preferentially promoting the most exothermic reactions.
  • No solvation, no surface catalysis. Prebiotic chemistry in aqueous solution involves hydration free energies and mineral-surface catalysis not captured here. The model represents an implicit-environment approximation appropriate for thermodynamic screening.
  • Discovery, not kinetics. The simulation identifies which reactions are thermodynamically accessible, not their rates or concentrations. The "steps" in the simulation are discovery events, not physical time.

Despite these approximations, the model's predictions are validated by their convergence with known prebiotic chemistry outcomes: the same amino acids, nucleobases, and metabolic intermediates that nature selected emerge as thermodynamic attractors (Section 3). This convergence provides post-hoc justification for the coarse-graining approach and establishes the model as a productive tool for prebiotic pathway hypothesis generation.

3.6 Synthesis Route Analysis: Histamine

To demonstrate the framework's practical utility for synthesis pathway design, we analyze the FM-ABM's discovery of 8 independent synthesis routes to histamine (C5H9N3), all starting from bare atoms. Routes vary in synthesis depth (4–5 steps), thermodynamic driving force (−272 to −559 kJ/mol for the final step), and topology (linear vs. convergent).

Key finding: The FM-ABM discovered all routes without any predefined reaction templates, retrosynthetic rules, or training data. Each route represents a thermodynamically viable pathway that could be further validated by DFT or experimental synthesis.

All discovered structures are imine-based (C=N bonds) rather than amine-based (C–NH2), reflecting the thermodynamic preference for double bonds. The classical histamine structure (imidazole ring + ethylamine) would require ring aromatization and amine bond formation — features that are kinetically, not thermodynamically, favored.

3.6.6 Recommendations

For laboratory synthesis

Route R4 (convergent, 4 steps). Prepare CH3N and C4H8N2 in parallel, combine in the final step. Shortest total synthesis, all intermediates are stable imines.

Next step: Validate each step with DFT transition state calculations (~10 CPU-hours per step = ~40 hours total, vs. 5 minutes for this screening).

For industrial scale-up

Route R6 (ammonia route, 5 steps). Uses NH3 as nitrogen source — cheapest and most available. Strongest driving force ensures high yield per step.

Next step: Process simulation with reaction kinetics to estimate actual yields and selectivity.

Comparison with existing retrosynthesis tools: Tools like ASKCOS (MIT), AiZynthFinder (AstraZeneca), and Synthia (Merck) discover synthesis routes using reaction template databases trained on millions of known reactions. Our FM-ABM discovers routes de novo from thermodynamic principles alone — no templates, no training data. This makes it uniquely suited for:
  • Novel chemical space where reaction databases have poor coverage
  • Prebiotic chemistry where no biological catalysts exist
  • First-pass screening to identify promising routes before expensive DFT validation
  • Teaching — students can watch the free market discover chemistry in real time

3.7 Temperature-Dependent Pathway Accessibility

The Evans-Polanyi kinetic barrier (Section 2.5) introduces a physically meaningful temperature dependence. We compare three reactor temperatures using the same 900-atom setup:

TemperatureMax Depth AccessibleSpecies DiscoveredWeakly Exothermic RoutesKey Effect
T = 288 K2789SuppressedOnly strongly exothermic steps proceed reliably

Key finding: At T = 400 K, depth-3 synthesis routes open up that are inaccessible at 288 K, because the Boltzmann factor exp(−Ea/kT) increases sufficiently for weakly exothermic reactions (|ΔE| ~ 50–100 kJ/mol) to proceed with meaningful probability. This is consistent with hydrothermal vent temperatures (350–450 K) proposed as optimal environments for prebiotic chemistry. At T = 600 K, even deeper routes become available but the system risks combinatorial explosion as the kinetic filter becomes too permissive.

The temperature effect is not simply "more reactions at higher T" — it is a qualitative change in the topology of accessible chemical space. At 288 K, the system explores a shallow landscape dominated by strongly exothermic C=N and C=O bond formations. At 400 K, new branches open through moderately exothermic C–C condensations, enabling convergent multi-step pathways that were kinetically blocked at lower temperatures.

3.8 Assembly Theory Validation

Assembly Theory (AT) proposes a framework for detecting selection in molecular systems by examining the relationship between structural complexity and abundance[7]. Its core constructs are:

  • Assembly index (ai): the minimum number of recursive joining steps needed to construct an object from basic building blocks.
  • Copy number (ni): how many copies of an object exist in a sample.
  • Formation history: the unique pathway of recursive construction steps that defines each object.

AT's central equation quantifies the assembly of an ensemble:

A = Sigma e^(a_i) x (n_i - 1) / N_T

where the sum runs over all species, ai is the assembly index, ni the copy number, and NT the total object count.

AT's key prediction: High assembly index combined with high copy number is a signature of selection. Random (undirected) processes produce monotonically declining copy numbers as assembly index increases. Only selection can sustain or amplify high-index objects.

Our FM-ABM provides an explicit mechanism for the selection that AT measures. Where AT detects selection after the fact, the FM-ABM implements it in real time through free-market dynamics: demand cascades, competitive pricing, and resource allocation by economic agents.

In the FM-ABM, the free market achieves this through GFCF demand cascades. When final demand targets a complex molecule, intermediate products are consumed to produce it, creating selective amplification of specific high-depth products at the expense of generic low-depth ones. The market does not amplify all high-depth molecules — only those on the supply chains demanded by GFCF. This is precisely the selectivity that AT's α parameter captures.

3.8.5 Key Implications

First explicit mechanism for AT's selection parameter. The FM-ABM is, to our knowledge, the first computational system that provides an explicit, tuneable mechanism for Assembly Theory's selection parameter. AT measures selection after the fact by analyzing molecular ensembles; the FM-ABM implements selection in real time through economic agents that allocate resources, generate demand, and compete for profit.

Three regimes from a single parameter. AT's three τd/τp regimes — stagnation, selection, and tar — emerge naturally from varying discoveryBudget, a single integer parameter. No additional tuning is required. This confirms that AT's phase diagram reflects a fundamental property of combinatorial discovery under resource constraints.

From measurement to mechanism. Assembly Theory provides a powerful analytical lens: given an ensemble of molecules, it can determine whether selection has acted. The FM-ABM complements this by providing the generative side: given a set of atoms and market rules, it produces the ensembles that AT can then analyze. Together, they form a complete loop from mechanism to measurement and back.

4.2 Why Does the Free Market Work?

Parallel exploration

900 agents simultaneously explore different regions of chemical space, sharing discoveries through trade networks. This is massively parallel combinatorial search without any central coordinator.

Demand cascades

When a complex molecule is demanded, the supply chain automatically pulls all intermediates — backward chaining from products to available reactants. The market "figures out" multi-step synthesis.

Thermodynamic selection

Only exothermic reactions (ΔE < 0) succeed, naturally pruning the vast combinatorial space. The market searches the chemical energy landscape the way evolution searches fitness landscapes.

Emergent specialization

Workers self-organize into supply chains: some produce methane, others ethane, others combine them into larger molecules. No central planning — just local interactions and energy minimization.

4.3 The SMILES Caveat

Important limitation: The molecules produced have the correct atomic formulas but not necessarily the correct structural isomers. Our "glycine" (C2H5NO2) has SMILES N=CCOO (an imine-peroxide), not NCC(=O)O (the classical α-amino acid).

This occurs because C=N (615 kJ/mol) is thermodynamically favored over C–N (305 kJ/mol). In prebiotic chemistry, tautomerization and environment-dependent rearrangements would interconvert between isomers. Our model captures the thermodynamic accessibility of the formula, which is the relevant quantity for predicting prebiotic amino acid abundance.

On the other hand, many smaller molecules emerge with the correct structure: formaldehyde appears as C=O, acetaldehyde as CC=O, and formic acid as O=CO — all exactly matching their real-world SMILES. The histamine analysis (Section 3.6) further illustrates this: while the biological isomer (imidazole ring + ethylamine) is not the thermodynamic product, the model correctly identifies the formula as accessible and discovers 8 independent synthesis routes to it. This provides a natural starting point for further investigation: once the FM-ABM identifies which formulas are thermodynamically accessible, ab initio quantum chemistry can determine the preferred structural isomers at a fraction of the cost of blind exploration.

4.4 Thermodynamic Inevitability

Our results support the hypothesis that amino acids are chemical inevitabilities, not biological inventions. The 10 "early" amino acids found by our simulation match those identified independently by:

1953 — Miller-Urey spark discharge experiments
1969 — Murchison meteorite amino acid analysis
2000 — Phylogenetic reconstruction of the genetic code (Trifonov)
2009 — Thermodynamic free energy rankings (Higgs & Pudritz)
2026 — Free-market agent-based model (this work)

Five completely independent lines of evidence converge on the same set. The genetic code's amino acid alphabet was dictated by thermodynamics, not evolutionary contingency. Nature chose these amino acids because they were the most readily available — the thermodynamic winners of prebiotic chemistry.

4.5 Practical Applications

The FM-ABM framework is not merely a historical reconstruction tool — it offers immediate practical applications for modern chemistry:

Novel synthesis pathway design

As demonstrated with histamine (Section 3.6), the FM-ABM discovers multiple independent routes to a target molecule in minutes, complete with energy profiles, supply chain trees, and multi-criteria rankings. This is orders of magnitude faster than DFT-based retrosynthesis and requires no reaction template databases.

Temperature optimization

The temperature-dependent analysis (Section 3.7) shows how varying reactor temperature opens or closes specific pathway families. This provides rapid screening for optimal synthesis conditions before committing to expensive laboratory experiments.

Retrosynthesis planning

The convergent route topology discovered for histamine (Route R4) directly maps to parallel synthesis strategies used in industrial chemistry. The FM-ABM identifies these strategies from first principles, without relying on human expertise or known reaction databases.

Prebiotic and astrochemistry

For environments where no biological catalysts exist (early Earth, meteorites, interstellar medium), template-based retrosynthesis tools are inapplicable. The FM-ABM fills this gap by operating purely from thermodynamic principles.

6. Conclusions

We have built a computational "time machine" that replays the first chapter of molecular evolution. Starting from nothing but atoms and thermodynamic rules, a free-market agent-based model spontaneously discovers life's complete molecular toolkit: 12 amino acids (appearing 14× earlier than random chance), all 5 nucleobases, the formose sugar chain (formaldehyde → ribose → glucose), Krebs cycle intermediates, nucleotide ring precursors, and the first tripeptide. The approach is 4–6 orders of magnitude cheaper than existing computational methods and provides not just products but complete synthesis networks with multiple independent pathways. Practical utility is demonstrated through the discovery of 8 independent synthesis routes to histamine, with full energy diagrams, supply chain analysis, and multi-criteria ranking directly applicable to laboratory and industrial synthesis planning. Temperature-dependent analysis reveals that hydrothermal conditions (400 K) open depth-3 pathways inaccessible at ambient temperature, consistent with the hydrothermal vent hypothesis for the origin of life. The model quantitatively validates Assembly Theory's predictions: three regimes (stagnation, selection, tar) emerge from varying a single parameter, and non-monotonic copy-number distributions provide the first explicit computational mechanism for AT's selection signature. The result suggests that the emergence of life's molecular toolkit was not a matter of chance but of thermodynamic necessity — and that simple darwinian economic optimization principles (supply, demand, competition) capture the essential dynamics of prebiotic chemical evolution.
